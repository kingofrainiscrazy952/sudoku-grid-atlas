## 🧭 Overview

SudokuVerse Trainer is a thoughtful, research-flavored practice companion for the classic 9×9 Sudoku grid. Where the original concept offers a trainer for practicing puzzles, this project takes a wider lens: it treats Sudoku as an entire *subject area* to be explored, measured, and understood. Think of it less as a puzzle app and more as an observatory for logic.

Every Sudoku that has ever existed lives somewhere on a spectrum — from the trivially gentle to the stubbornly opaque, from puzzles with a single elegant solution to grids that hide multiple truths, and occasionally to grids that hold no solution at all. This trainer maps that spectrum. It gives players and researchers the tools to wander the full terrain of 9×9 Sudoku, not just the well-lit paths.

Whether you are a curious newcomer learning your first naked single, a competitive solver chasing sub-minute times, or a mathematician probing the boundaries of uniqueness and solvability, SudokuVerse Trainer gives you the instruments to look deeper.

## 🎯 Why This Project Exists

Most Sudoku software does one thing: it shows you a grid and waits. SudokuVerse Trainer asks a different question — *what is the shape of the entire Sudoku universe?*

By offering dedicated spaces for **solvable**, **ambiguous**, and **unsolvable** puzzles, the app reframes Sudoku from a casual pastime into a genuine intellectual playground. The unsolvable grids are not bugs; they are teachers. The ambiguous grids are not failures; they are lessons in under-determination. Understanding why a puzzle cannot be solved is often more illuminating than solving one that can.

## 🏛️ The Sudoku Atlas Explained

The Atlas is the heart of this project. It is a structured, browsable map of the Sudoku domain, divided into three primary continents:

1. **The Solvable Continent** — grids with exactly one completion. These are the everyday puzzles, but the Atlas sorts them by the deepest technique required to crack them open. A grid that falls to a single naked single is a different species from one that demands advanced chains.
2. **The Ambiguous Archipelago** — grids with two or more valid solutions. Here, the Atlas highlights the exact cells where the puzzle becomes a fork in the road.
3. **The Contradiction Wastes** — grids with no solution at all. Each entry ships with a machine-generated proof sketch showing the earliest logical conflict.

Navigating the Atlas builds intuition. Over time you begin to *feel* the shape of a puzzle before you place your first digit.

## 🖥️ A Responsive Experience

The interface was rebuilt around a mobile-first philosophy and then scaled upward. On a phone, controls collapse into a compact thumb-reachable strip. On a tablet, side panels emerge for notes and the Atlas. On a desktop, the full three-column workspace (grid, atlas, telemetry) unfolds comfortably. No feature is ever hidden behind a device.

## 🚀 Getting Started (Conceptual)

Shipping this project to your machine is deliberately friendly. There are three routes, none of which involve command-line gymnastics:

1. **The Web Portal** — open the hosted experience and begin solving instantly in a browser.
2. **The Desktop Bundle** — a self-contained package for offline use on major desktop platforms.
3. **The Mobile Companion** — installable to your home screen or app drawer for on-the-go practice.

Once running, the first-run wizard walks you through difficulty selection, language choice, and whether you want the Atlas visible by default. Ten seconds later, you are solving.

## 🛠️ Technology Snapshot

The stack favors clarity and longevity. A typed core handles grid representation and solving. A lightweight reactive layer drives the UI.
